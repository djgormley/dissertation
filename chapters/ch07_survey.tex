\chapter{The Archive Dataset: Captures, Reprocessing, \& Products}
\label{sec:survey}

The survey is the bridge between a verified detector and a scientific claim.
It applies the detector to archival telescope data, preserves enough intermediate
information to revisit the decision later, and records where the archive cannot
support an inference.  This chapter therefore distinguishes three objects that
are easy to conflate: the \emph{available archive}, the \emph{processing
inventory}, and the \emph{population of telescope time} about which one might
want to make a statement.  The first two are measurable from logs.  The third
requires a selection model, because the archive consists of triggered snapshots
rather than a uniform observing stream.

\section{Triggered Baseband Captures and Their Selection Function}
\label{sec:survey:data}

The raw material is CHIME's archive of full-array baseband captures recorded by
the telescope's transient backend \cite{amiri2018frb}.  A capture contains the
packed $4{+}4$-bit channelized stream for all $M = 2048$ inputs and spans
seconds rather than a continuous day.  The survey enumerates every archived
event, excludes those carrying the recorded outrigger label, and targets the
remainder on all 23 ATSC allocations (channels 14--36), each complete against
the archive holdings at the survey snapshot.  Three denominators are kept
separate throughout, because ``discovered and readable'' is not the same
population as ``contributed a detector frame'': the inventory of unique
event--channel source objects, the processed product units, and the stored
detector frames.  Units shorter than one complete $N = 16384$-sample frame are
retained in the ledger as zero-frame units rather than dropped.  The
stored-frame population has a median of a few $N$-sample detector frames per
acquisition, and its calendar coverage runs from \rerun{December 2018} to the
snapshot.  Per-channel event counts are far from uniform, and the deficit is
concentrated on the most contaminated channels, for the selection reason
discussed below.  Those figures describe the working archive at snapshot time;
the immutable release ledger, rather than this prose, is the authoritative
denominator.

Frame validity is decided at processing time by reason-coded input-health rules
that are independent of the detection statistic: an all-zero frame, a frame
whose every decoded sample sits at the same rail (a constant word at negative
full scale is the signature of an acquisition-side fault, not of receiver
noise), a frame with an anomalous decoded code histogram, and a frame whose
reference denominator is degenerate are each recorded with their reason and
excluded from every scientific denominator.  The exclusion is part of the
product, so the valid population is a stated object rather than an implicit one.

The captures were not selected by the DTV detector.  They were initiated by the
transient system and filtered by telescope operations, data availability,
archive retention, and later inventory rules.  One of those filters is measured
and deserves emphasis because it is \emph{endogenous to the contamination
itself}: the telescope curtails baseband collection on its most contaminated
coarse channels, and captures on the channel-30 monitored bin cease entirely
after \rerun{September 2023} --- an operations exclusion driven by the DTV
occupancy this survey measures.  Archive completeness is therefore correlated
with the quantity under study; the thin channel-30 and channel-24 inventories
are a selection effect, not a property of the transmitters, and a channel
excluded from collection can never report a subsequent transmitter sign-off
from this archive (several sign-offs or station departures were recorded on
collected channels during the survey span, so the possibility is not
hypothetical).

Let $I(t,c)$ be the indicator that telescope time $t$ on channel $c$ enters the
survey.  The occupancy quantity measured directly by the survey is
\begin{equation}
\Pr(\text{DTV state} \mid I = 1, c),
\end{equation}
not the unconditional time occupancy $\Pr(\text{DTV state} \mid c)$ unless the
inclusion process is ignorable for the variables controlling propagation and
transmitter state.  Time of day, season, sidereal time, weather, trigger class,
commissioning state, and station-era boundaries are all plausible dependencies.
The archive can still support a strong detector and calibration study, but its
masked fractions are explicitly \emph{archive} fractions until the selection
comparison is completed.

The exposure profiles keep a second denominator because a stored frame is not
automatically a timed frame: frames without a finite timestamp and a positive
finite duration remain in count-based summaries but contribute no invented
seconds to an exposure histogram.  The release tabulates frame counts and
durations by UTC month and hour, America/Vancouver civil month, hour, and
meteorological season, and local mean sidereal hour.  The last coordinate uses
the declared DRAO longitude ($-119.6175^\circ$) and UTC as a documented proxy
for UT1; it is therefore a reproducible coverage diagnostic, not an
astrometry-grade apparent-sidereal timestamp.  Trigger and scheduled labels are
measured inventory fields.  Operations states that were never recorded remain
unavailable rather than being inferred from those labels.

Two consequences recur later.  First, the snapshot duration caps any contiguous
block statistic that can be reproduced from this archive at the longest capture
(\rerun{$1.38$~s}).  Second, the sparse spacing leaves a correlation-time blind
interval between the frame duration and typical acquisition separation.
Chapter~\ref{sec:calibration} therefore allows the correlation estimator to
return a measurement, a bound, or a refusal rather than interpolating across
that gap.

\section{A Restartable, Bounded-Storage Survey}
\label{sec:survey:trawl}

The survey processes every eligible capture through the same detector arithmetic
described in Chapters~\ref{sec:pipeline} and~\ref{sec:impl}.  It is organized in
three nested units: an inventory entry identifies an archived acquisition, a
processing unit identifies a contiguous readable segment, and a detector frame
is the $N$-sample decision quantum.  This separation allows the operational
machinery to restart without changing the scientific definition of a frame.

The processing environment cannot stage the entire archive locally, so the
survey runs a bounded-storage cycle --- discover, pull one unit, validate,
process every complete frame, write and verify a content-addressed product,
then release the staged source --- with failed units quarantined with their
exception and provenance rather than silently omitted, and with resume keyed on
the source identity, detector geometry, weight and transform hashes, schema,
and science-relevant configuration, so that a kernel, geometry, or schema
change creates a new cohort while a cosmetic source change does not force a
re-run.  The operational details (retry, checkpoint, staging, and deletion
rules) are repository documentation, not science.

The survey is intentionally detector-agnostic at the operational layer, and
its two layers live in two tools.  \texttt{datatrail} --- CHIME/FRB's archive
data-management client, in the fork that carries the recursive discovery,
inventory-manifest, manifest-pull-with-integrity, dataset-verification, and
doctor commands added for this work \cite{datatrail-cli} --- owns the archive
side: discovery of every eligible capture, the inventory manifest, transfer
with integrity checks against CANFAR's storage, and dataset verification.  The
\texttt{pilot-proxy} archive runtime drives it and owns everything else:
survey state and restart, retry and checkpoint, quarantine, control, and the
analyzer that consumes a frame and emits the declared product schema, binding
each processed unit to its archive version, receiver software tag, and typed
input-map digest.  This boundary is why failures in
archive access do not become hidden branches in the signal-processing code, and
why the same survey core can support other large telescope reductions.  Three
verdict types are kept distinct in the survey loop: a transient failure is
retried, a contract refusal (a source object that does not satisfy the declared
input contract) is recorded and skipped, and an event with no data is accepted
as empty.  The survey stage records its own denominator --- every discovered
unit, its archive-read and formula-derived fields, and every resume through an
uncontrolled host restart --- so that a later re-survey can be compared with it
field by field.

\section{Products: Statistics, Not Only Verdicts}
\label{sec:survey:products}

Each product (schema \texttt{pilotproxy\_per\_pilot\_product\_v5}) retains, per
frame: the three exact \texttt{uint64} fine-power spectra $S_r[f]$ over every
fine bin, so that any later rank $\rho$ can be evaluated; the exact coarse
integer power terms $P_r$; the frame's UTC timestamp, duration, and event and
acquisition identifiers, which are the keys every blocked split and exposure
table is built on; the reason-coded validity state; the active-input count and
the separate railed-component and missing-fill counts against a shared
denominator; the per-frame survey flag of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:survey-bootstrap-rule}, a mechanism check rather than a decision; and the per-frame feed-summed power spectrum on the frame's own $N$-point
grid ($23.8$~Hz bins across the coarse channel), stored as \texttt{int16}
codes at $0.01$~dB per step about a per-frame reference level and decoded to
linear power before any average is formed --- so the masked and unmasked
spectra of any later $(\rho,\eta)$ are assembled by averaging decoded frames,
and no sub-frame time structure or raw voltage is retained.  The reference
marginals $P_1$ and $P_2$ and the decoded mean input power together serve as
the per-frame receiver gain and noise proxy; the product records them for that
purpose rather than assuming the receiver stationary across an era.  Product-wide metadata retain
the source identity, weight-bank and transform hashes, resolved reference
placement, exact integer norms, detector and schema versions, the receiver
software and typed input-map identity of every source unit, and the
within-channel time-averaged spectra used for the broadcast characterization.
Per-unit arrays are indexed by the recorded processing order; the parallel
sorted key listing is not the alignment axis, and the alignment is verified
directly against the archive's own object sizes and path dates.
Figure~\ref{fig:survey:product} draws one product.

What the retained integers do and do not remove should be stated plainly.
They remove reconstruction ambiguity, any assumed fine-stage sensitivity
credit, and any uncertainty about how a mask was computed.  They do not remove
receiver-gain variation, the pilot-to-shelf calibration, PFB and straddle loss,
the pilot-to-allocation transfer, or the scalar-power-to-visibility transfer;
each of those remains an explicit gate in Chapters~\ref{sec:estimator}
and~\ref{sec:tolerance}.  This choice makes every threshold exactly reversible.  A product that stored
only a mask bit could answer what one historical policy rejected; the retained
integer terms instead permit any later coarse rule to be re-evaluated by the
same exact cross-product, and any later fine pair $(\rho,\eta_{q16})$ to be
replayed exactly from the three integer fine powers --- with no gain or loss
assumption and no return to the raw capture.  No decision is baked into the products: the stored flag is the mechanism
check of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:decision}, and the fine order-statistic
decision is applied to the products after calibration (Chapter~\ref{sec:calibration}).  Implementation verification and policy are thus
kept separate without a floating diagnostic ever standing in for the decision
operands.

\section{Reproduction and the Required Denominator}
\label{sec:survey:reproduction}

A reproduction claim about the survey is only as good as its denominator, and
the release states it in four parts:
\begin{itemize}
\item the number of discovered, eligible, and successfully joined events and frames;
\item join completeness and the treatment of duplicates, missing products, and invalid frames;
\item the exact fields and byte representations compared; and
\item the source, binary, toolkit, weight, transform, schema, and cohort hashes.
\end{itemize}
The survey stage carries such a denominator for discovery: a re-survey of the
live archive is compared with the frozen inventory unit by unit, and every
archive-read and formula-derived field --- object sizes, common paths,
observation dates, frame estimates --- must agree, with archive growth in the
interval reported separately and no unit lost.  The processing stage carries it
for the products: the cross-build comparison of Section~\ref{sec:impl:verify}
joins two independently built cohorts on the physical archive key and compares
their integer outputs.  Absent telemetry is reported as absent: throughput,
transfer volume, GPU time, peak staging storage, and wall time are recorded only
where the survey logs preserve them, and catalogued bytes are never relabelled
as transferred bytes.

\section{Selection-Aware Calibration and Holdout Design}
\label{sec:survey:holdout}

Randomly splitting individual frames would overstate independence: neighboring
frames within a capture share receiver state, transmitter state, and propagation,
and frames across a station era can share a persistent offset.  Calibration and
evaluation are therefore blocked at the acquisition or sidereal-day level and
stratified by measured station era.  The latest accepted stable era is the input
population for a current policy; its dates and label remain provenance rather
than threshold inputs.

Within that era, one block set performs preparation: it rejects invalid frames,
localizes the anchor and usable bulk, resolves the floor and correlation treatment,
applies the declared residual transfers, and tests block stability.  Only an
authorized preparation emits the complete mapping of candidate $\rho$ values to
residual-score histograms.  Each bin records a frame count, a calibrated
$r_{\rm sys}$ total, and an $r_{\rm var}$ total when statistical-cost calibration
is available.  The selector receives only this mapping and the science tolerance;
it derives sample count and masked fraction from bin counts and returns
$(\rho,\eta)$.  A missing or refused preparation quantity prevents authorization
of the histogram rather than becoming another selector argument.

Disjoint blocks then estimate false alarms, masked fraction, residual levels, and
cost for the frozen pair.  A stricter leave-one-year-or-era-out check asks whether
the selected operating point transports across calendar structure.

The holdout design cannot make the archive uniform, but it prevents a second,
avoidable optimism: evaluating a threshold on the same correlated frames that
selected it.  The final campaign reports both the within-archive blocked result
and the coverage diagnostics of Table~\ref{tab:survey:inventory}.  Only then can
an archive fraction be promoted to an estimate of observing-time occupancy, and
only over the population for which the coverage comparison supports that move.

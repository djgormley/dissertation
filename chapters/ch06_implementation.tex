
\chapter{Thresholded Detection: Decision Rules, Exact Implementation, \& Verification}
\label{sec:detection}
\label{sec:impl}

This chapter follows the same four levels as Chapter~\ref{sec:estimator}: mathematical policy, synthetic calibration, offline archive evaluation, and online shadow operation. Detection begins where threshold-free estimation ends. Its primary science trade is surviving contamination against masked exposure; false-alarm and detection probabilities remain supporting diagnostics.

\section{Detection Mathematics and the Science Decision}
\label{sec:detection:rule}
\label{sec:pipeline:decision}

Chapter~\ref{sec:estimator} defines and validates the estimator without applying a threshold. Detection begins only when an estimator output is compared with a calibrated decision boundary. This separation matters: transfer accuracy asks whether the statistic measures the injected excess, whereas the science decision asks how much exposure must be masked to keep the surviving contamination within tolerance. A conventional receiver-operating curve can diagnose threshold behavior when truth-labelled on/off trials exist, but it does not by itself price the residual left in the kept data.

\subsection*{Measured departure from the i.i.d.\ model}

Empirical system measurements reveal that the idealized model's tails cannot be trusted for threshold setting. On the one transmitter-off cohort available when the design was fixed (channel 35's pre-switch-on era), the observed coarse null was \rerun{${\sim}19\times$} wider than the i.i.d.\ $F$-model, while the fine per-bin null exhibited a near-ideal core contaminated by a heavy \rerun{${\sim}0.3\%$} tail that inflated its width factor to \rerun{${\sim}13.8\times$}. Both factors are raw standard-deviation ratios against the analytic i.i.d.\ widths; they are quoted here to motivate the design, not as portable constants. Width factors of this kind are sensitive to the estimator and the cohort --- a robust core-width estimate of the same nulls sits an order of magnitude lower, precisely because the tail carries the width --- so Chapter~\ref{sec:calibration} re-derives them for every channel on its current era under a single pinned definition set (estimator, transient handling, cohort), reporting the robust-core equivalents alongside (Table~\ref{tab:calibration:nulls}). The physical origins are receiver systematics --- gain variations across the frame, spectral leakage from features elsewhere in the channel, and non-Gaussian interference --- none of which the i.i.d.\ derivation models.

Two design consequences follow. First, thresholds must be \emph{calibrated from measured nulls} rather than computed from the $F$ survival function; the analytic distribution of Section~\ref{sec:estimator:statistics} is retained as the idealized baseline and as a lower bound on achievable width, not as the operating calibration. Second, the fine null's clean core and thin contaminated tail favor an order statistic over a moment-based background estimate. An extreme value can drag a mean or variance, while a fixed rank places a finite one-sided bound on the effect of a limited number of upward-contaminated bins.

\textit{Remark: why there is no taper.} Both stages of the reduction are deliberately untapered. Within the $K$-tap profile, uniform magnitude is the matched filter for a tone of known frequency: any taper trades deflection for sidelobe suppression --- a Hann profile forfeits $1.8$~dB --- and sensitivity is the scarce resource for a beacon $11.3$~dB below its own shelf. Along the window axis, doubling the hypothesis grid bounds the worst-case straddle loss near $0.9$~dB without the sensitivity cost and wider designated window of a taper. Leakage from structure elsewhere in the coarse channel is instead addressed by the local ratio, reference placement, guard and census exclusions, and the ranked background estimator below. Any taper would also create a new weight bank, null calibration, and survey epoch.

\subsection*{Which axis decides, and which one checks}

The coarse and fine axes are not independent detectors. Under the declared known-tone, common-calibration model, the deflection identity of Section~\ref{sec:estimator:statistics} shows that the fine axis concentrates the same signal energy against $L$ times fewer null degrees of freedom and improves idealized deflection by $\sqrt{L}$. This is a model result, not a universal dominance theorem: mismatch, nonstationary backgrounds, and different calibration errors can change empirical ordering. The proposed runtime design therefore assigns detection to the fine axis only after out-of-sample calibration.

The archive survey could not begin with measured per-channel anchors and nulls because producing those measurements was one purpose of the survey --- and it did not need to: calibration selects its operating point afterwards from the retained integers, so no threshold is required while the archive is processed. The survey does carry one calibration-free coarse flag, evaluated per frame not as a decision but as a \emph{mechanism check}: proof, on every frame, that the coarse path executed and the marginal identity held, and a crude occupancy indicator besides. It fires when the target profile carries more power than the mean of its references:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:survey-bootstrap-rule}
\text{flag} \iff F > \mu_0,
\qquad
F = \frac{2 P_0}{P_1 + P_2},
\qquad
\mu_0 = \frac{2\|\mathbf{w}_0\|^2}{\|\mathbf{w}_1\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}_2\|^2}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mu_0$ is the exact rational norm correction of Section~\ref{sec:param:refs}, not a tuned threshold. It is the ratio-of-expected-powers scale under white, isotropic noise, not the value a random realization takes and not the exact finite-sample mean of $F$. Cancelling the common factor of two gives the integer comparison
\begin{equation}
P_0\bigl(\|\mathbf{w}_1\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}_2\|^2\bigr)
>
\|\mathbf{w}_0\|^2(P_1 + P_2),
\end{equation}
which forms no quotient and contains no empirically calibrated constant. The survey product records this flag per frame with threshold mode \emph{none}; it is not a policy, and because the product retains the integer terms, any coarse or fine rule --- including the selected operating point --- is applied to the same frames exactly, after the fact.

Used as a policy, that flag would be ruinous --- it fires on any positive excess, and therefore on about \rerun{half} of verified-quiet frames (\rerun{$44$--$49\%$} on the transmitter-off cohorts measured so far) --- which is exactly why it is not one; its flag rate is quoted below only as an occupancy indicator, never as a masked fraction. The coarse-versus-fine comparison on the same frames shows why the fine axis decides: at null $P_{\rm fa}=0.05$, the fine designated-set rule holds \rerun{$P_d \geq 0.997$} on channels where the calibrated coarse points fall to \rerun{$0.06$ and $0.70$}. These are archive measurements of particular cohorts, not the operating curve of the synthetic campaign of Section~\ref{sec:detection:synthetic}.

Section~\ref{sec:tolerance:thresholds} selects the per-channel operating point with RFIsher against the science tolerance from each channel's current-era null distribution. Every masked fraction quoted from the survey is at that selected $(\rho^\star,\eta^\star)$, applied after the fact to the retained integers, and the threshold stays configuration data rather than a frozen constant. Where the coarse and fine multipliers must be distinguished, the coarse one is written $\eta_c$ (the rule $F > \eta_c \mu_0$) and the fine one $\eta$.

\subsection*{The fine decision and its calibration}

The fine statistic runs on every processed archive frame, and because the product retains its three exact integer power spectra, the fine rule can be applied to the archive exactly as it is applied live. In the runtime design it is the sole decision path. The coarse marginal remains as an inexpensive diagnostic and as the Parseval tripwire of Section~\ref{sec:impl:verify}; it does not supply the live verdict.

One policy statement belongs here because two levels of the detector are retuned differently. The coarse weights and the fine bulk are \emph{nominal and fixed}: the synthesized target is the ATSC pilot at its standardized offset, and no measured line moves it. The designated window is the \emph{one} era-specific element: it is centred on the measured anchor $f_a$ of Section~\ref{sec:calibration:anchors}, because the residual offset is a property of the transmitter rather than of the standard, and it is carried as bundle data with the era's provenance rather than as a change to the weight bank. Measured offsets therefore validate $K$ and place the window; they never retune the projection. The fine rule belongs to the ordered-statistic branch of the Constant False Alarm Rate family \cite{rohling1983}, but ``constant'' is a calibrated, model-conditional property rather than an algebraic guarantee. Normalization by a same-frame background statistic follows common changes in scale. The achieved false-alarm probability remains constant only when the calibration null is exchangeable with the operating null after normalization, including its bulk composition, dependence, and tail shape. A change in null regime therefore triggers recalibration, and performance must be measured on a blocked off-epoch holdout rather than inferred from the name of the method.

Sort the $|\mathcal{B}|$ background values in ascending order and denote the $\rho$-th value by $T_{(\rho)}$. Its normalized rank is $q_\rho=\rho/(|\mathcal{B}|+1)$, the convention used to compare channels with different usable bulk sizes. As a one-sided replacement bound, if $m$ clean values are replaced by arbitrarily large contaminants and $m \le |\mathcal{B}|-\rho$, the result lies between the original clean $T_{(\rho)}$ and $T_{(\rho+m)}$; beyond that headroom it can be driven without bound. Thus $|\mathcal{B}|-\rho$ counts upward-breakdown headroom, not replacements that leave the numerical value unchanged.

The archive's per-bin statistic supports this calibration directly. Measured pilot anchors lie \rerun{hundreds of hertz to $1.4$~kHz} from nominal, inside the $\pm1.5$~kHz fine span (Table~\ref{tab:calibration:anchors}). The exchangeability the rank rule requires is testable: on quiet frames, a bulk bin tested against the rank of the remaining bulk must exceed it at the combinatorial rate $(|\mathcal{B}|+1-\rho)/(|\mathcal{B}|+1)$; on channel 29's quiet frames it did so to three significant figures at every tested rank. That check is repeated per channel on the current era (Table~\ref{tab:calibration:nulls}). Measured anchor changes between station eras make the resulting calibration a channel--era object.

Let $f_a$ be the measured pilot anchor. The designated set spans the padded main lobe,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D} = \{ (f_a + k) \bmod L_F : |k| \le 2 \}.
\end{equation}
The usable null bulk $\mathcal{B}$ contains alternate bins from the independent subset of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:coherent}, excludes $\mathcal{D}$ and any census-marked persistent features, and omits bins with zero reference denominator. An even-parity anchor leaves $125$ bulk bins and an odd-parity anchor leaves $126$. The latter case leaves the pilot's $\pm3$-padded-bin shoulders in the independent bulk; the selected rank's stated headroom bounds their upward influence, or a calibration can census-exclude them explicitly. The survey's recorded diagnostic background used a different broad scaffolding before per-channel anchors were known; it is not the runtime bulk mask defined here.

With $\hat{T}_{\mathrm{null}}=T_{(\rho)}$ over that bulk, the proposed decision is
\begin{equation}
\text{reject } H_0 \iff \max_{f \in \mathcal{D}} T[f] > \eta\,\hat{T}_{\mathrm{null}}.
\end{equation}
The statistical detection floor is the smallest multiplier that meets a declared empirical $P_{\rm fa}$ on verified transmitter-off epochs. The science threshold is instead the multiplier required by the contamination-residual tolerance of Chapter~\ref{sec:tolerance}. The two need not be equal, and a coarse multiplier $\eta_c$ does not transfer numerically to the fine multiplier: the fine pair $(\rho,\eta)$ is selected on its own axis. Either multiplier enters its bundle as $\eta_{q16}=\lfloor\eta 2^{16}\rceil$ --- unity is the integer $65{,}536$ and the integer $1$ is the real multiplier $2^{-16}$ --- leaving the comparison exact and keeping policy in data rather than code.

The rule is scale-invariant because a bin-common factor, including the weight-norm factor $\mu_0$, cancels between the designated maximum and ranked background. Its one-sided contamination bound is explicit, and its rank and threshold comparisons admit exact integer cross-multiplication. Two degenerate rules fail safe toward non-rejection: a designated bin with zero reference denominator cannot fire, and a frame whose usable bulk is not deeper than $\rho$ is invalid and emits no rejection.

\subsection*{Tolerance-selected frontier}

Throughout, $\rho$ is the detector rank and nothing else; the residual shares of Chapter~\ref{sec:tolerance} are written $\varphi$. For valid frame or trial $i$, define the normalized decision statistic
\begin{align}
Z_i(\rho) &\equiv
\frac{\max_{f\in\mathcal D_i}T_i[f]}{T_{i,(\rho)}},\\
d_i(\rho,\eta) &\equiv
\mathbf 1\!\left\{Z_i(\rho)>\eta\right\}.
\end{align}
The operating-point selector does not consume these frame records directly. For each candidate rank, calibration reduces the latest stable era to a residual-score histogram
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_{\rho}
=\left(
|\mathcal B|,
\{b_{\rho k}\}_{k=1}^{J_\rho},
\{(n_{\rho j},S^{\rm sys}_{\rho j},[S^{\rm var}_{\rho j}])\}_{j=0}^{J_\rho}
\right).
\label{eq:detection:histogram}
\end{equation}
The first field records the usable bulk size and requires the one-based rank $1\le\rho\le|\mathcal B|$. The ordered $b_{\rho k}$ are the candidate values of $\eta$. Bin zero contains scores at or below $b_{\rho1}$, bin $j$ for $0<j<J_\rho$ contains scores in $(b_{\rho j},b_{\rho,j+1}]$, and bin $J_\rho$ is the required overflow above the last candidate. Thus every accepted equal-duration, equal-exposure frame occurs exactly once even though the overflow is not itself a candidate threshold. The field $n_{\rho j}$ is the count in bin $j$, and $S^{\rm sys}_{\rho j}$ is the sum of its already science-calibrated coherent-residual contributions. The optional $S^{\rm var}_{\rho j}$ field carries the corresponding variance-residual total. Its omission leaves $r_{\rm var}$ unavailable and declares a masking-only cost; the selector never infers it from the coherent term. Across ranks, the family must contain the same accepted frame population and usable bulk size, the same full coherent-residual total, and either omit variance throughout or carry the same full variance-residual total. These invariants make a fraction of frame counts a fraction of exposure and prevent unlike rank products from being compared.

For one such threshold, all required exposure and residual quantities follow from sums over the histogram:
\begin{align}
N_\rho &\equiv \sum_{j=0}^{J_\rho} n_{\rho j},
&K_\rho(b_{\rho k})&\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}n_{\rho j},\\
m(\rho,b_{\rho k})&\equiv1-\frac{K_\rho(b_{\rho k})}{N_\rho},
&r_{\rm sys}(\rho,b_{\rho k})&\equiv
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}S^{\rm sys}_{\rho j}}
{K_\rho(b_{\rho k})},\\
&&r_{\rm var}(\rho,b_{\rho k})&\equiv
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}S^{\rm var}_{\rho j}}
{K_\rho(b_{\rho k})}\quad\text{when the optional totals are present}.
\end{align}
Thus the sample count is the sum of the count bins, the kept count is a prefix sum, and the masked fraction requires no separate exposure input. A candidate with $K_\rho<30$ is not evaluated; the fixed population floor is derived from the histogram rather than supplied separately. The stored residual totals are sufficient only when each retained prefix sum divided by its count is already the calibrated residual for that subset. A transfer or correlation model that must be refit after masking cannot use this compression and remains in preparation.

The histogram is a calibrated science product, not a substitute for calibration. Invalid-frame rejection, selection of the latest stable era, anchor and bulk validation, correlation and coherence treatment, proxy-to-residual transfer, and stability and coverage checks all occur before it is constructed. A failed check prevents construction. Dates, rejected-frame logs, older eras, and the raw proxy distribution remain provenance and evidence outside the selector. Consequently its only scientific inputs are the accepted histogram family $\{\mathcal H_\rho\}$ and the channel's science tolerance $r_{\rm tol}$.

For each fixed $\rho$, sweeping $\eta$ traces an empirical curve with surviving residual on the horizontal axis and masked fraction on the vertical axis. Equivalently, invert that curve for a residual allowance $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
m_{\rho}^\star(\tau)
=\min_{\eta}\left\{m(\rho,\eta):
r_{\rm sys}(\rho,\eta)\le\tau\right\},
\qquad
m^\star(\tau)=\min_{\rho}m_{\rho}^\star(\tau).
\label{eq:detection:frontier}
\end{equation}
Thus $\rho$ indexes curves, $\eta$ labels measured points along each curve, and the science tolerance supplies the boundary. The empirical steps are retained without smoothing. This minimum-mask envelope is the clearest display of the trade, but the final operating point uses the full survey cost
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(\rho,\eta)
=\frac{1+r_{\rm var}(\rho,\eta)}{1-m(\rho,\eta)}
\end{equation}
over finite recorded candidates with $K_{\rho}(\eta)\ge30$ and $r_{\rm sys}(\rho,\eta)\le r_{\rm tol}$. Let $\mathcal C_{\min}$ be the smallest feasible cost and define the plateau
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P=\left\{(\rho,\eta):
\mathcal C(\rho,\eta)\le1.02\mathcal C_{\min}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The selected pair $(\rho^\star,\eta^\star)$ is the member of $\mathcal P$ with lexicographically smallest $(r_{\rm sys},m,\rho,\eta)$. This prevents bin-level numerical detail from turning a flat surface into a precise claim while keeping the function's scientific inputs limited to the histogram family and tolerance. The selected bundle converts $\eta$ to Q16 and is frozen before any holdout is opened; holdout processing applies that pair once and reports its realized mask and residual without rebuilding the selection histogram or retuning the pair.

\section{Exact Realization and Engineering Verification}

\subsection{The Exactness Contract}
\label{sec:impl:contract}
The implementation is governed by a single engineering contract: \emph{the production-candidate GPU datapath and an independent reference implementation must produce bit-identical outputs for identical inputs} --- not statistically consistent outputs, not outputs agreeing to a tolerance, but the same integers. This standard is unusual for scientific signal processing and is justified here in detail, since the remainder of this section depends on it.

Floating-point pipelines cannot meet it across implementations. Library FFTs on different platforms use different factorizations and operation orders; compilers contract multiply--add sequences into fused instructions at their discretion; parallel reductions reassociate sums nondeterministically. Two correct floating-point implementations of the same mathematics therefore differ in their final bits, and any comparison between them must adopt a tolerance. Tolerances carry three compounding costs in an unattended system: every verification gate becomes a statistical judgment; small systematic defects (an off-by-one window, a swapped conjugate, a biased rounding) can persist undetected within the tolerance; and when two eras of the system disagree, benign arithmetic drift cannot be distinguished from genuine regression. Exact integer arithmetic removes all three costs: equality is decidable per frame, and any defect that changes an \emph{exercised} output becomes exactly detectable at the first comparison it reaches.  This is an implementation guarantee, not a claim of complete test coverage or scientific correctness: an unexercised control path can still contain a defect, and bit equality cannot validate the signal, calibration, or cosmological transfer models.  Reproductions across supported hosts, compiler versions, and hardware generations are expected to be exact once the same frozen specification and artifact manifest are used, so a mismatched bit is unambiguous evidence of disagreement even though a match is only evidence for the cases exercised.

The scope of the contract is as follows. Every estimator quantity of Chapter~\ref{sec:estimator} --- the projections $\mathbf{Z}$, the spectra $\widetilde{B}_m$, and the power sums $S_r[f]$ and $P_r$ --- together with the mask decision of this chapter is computed in integer arithmetic with accumulator widths proven sufficient in Section~\ref{sec:param:tradeoffs}, so no intermediate value rounds, saturates, or overflows. The single point at which the computation departs from real-valued mathematics is the fixed-point FFT's butterfly rounding, and that departure is itself part of the frozen specification (Section~\ref{sec:impl:fxfft}): deterministic, portable, measured, and identical in every implementation. Floating point appears only in derived diagnostics: the archive records the coarse \texttt{fstat\_raw} quotient as \texttt{float64} and the per-bin \texttt{fstat\_fine} quotients as \texttt{float32}; neither dtype is upstream of a decision.

\subsection{The Packed Fixed-Point Datapath}
\label{sec:impl:datapath}
The detector consumes packed $4{+}4$-bit complex samples, one byte per sample, real component in the high nibble (Section~\ref{sec:system:instrument}). The receiver's native encoding is excess-8 (offset binary); the ingest adapter repacks each byte losslessly to two's complement (per byte, XOR 0x88), and the kernel unpacks by sign-extending each two's-complement nibble; weights are stored in the same packed format on the same $[-7, 7]$ grid the receiver's 15-level quantizer emits. The coordinate convention noted in Section~\ref{sec:param:target} is fixed here once for the whole system: the receiver's spectrally inverted channel ordering is absorbed by synthesizing weight tones with the conjugate (negative-exponent) rotation and time-reversing each detector window before projection; both choices are recorded as explicit fields of the runtime calibration bundle's detector contract, so the convention is auditable configuration data.

The projection stage --- the dominant cost identified in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:segmentation} --- runs on packed 8-bit dot-product instructions (DP4A), which accumulate four $8{\times}8$-bit products per instruction into a 32-bit register. The mapping from complex arithmetic onto these lanes requires care. Two consecutive packed samples unpack in registers into two lane words,
\[
a_{\mathrm{re}} = (x_{0,\mathrm{re}},\; x_{0,\mathrm{im}},\; x_{1,\mathrm{re}},\; x_{1,\mathrm{im}}), \qquad
a_{\mathrm{im}} = (x_{0,\mathrm{im}},\; -x_{0,\mathrm{re}},\; x_{1,\mathrm{im}},\; -x_{1,\mathrm{re}}),
\]
while the corresponding two weight taps are pre-packed once per weight upload into a single shared lane $w = (w_{0,\mathrm{re}},\; w_{0,\mathrm{im}},\; w_{1,\mathrm{re}},\; w_{1,\mathrm{im}})$. One DP4A of $a_{\mathrm{re}}$ against $w$ then accumulates $x_{\mathrm{re}}w_{\mathrm{re}} + x_{\mathrm{im}}w_{\mathrm{im}}$ for both taps --- the real part of $x\,\overline{w}$ --- and one DP4A of $a_{\mathrm{im}}$ against $w$ accumulates $x_{\mathrm{im}}w_{\mathrm{re}} - x_{\mathrm{re}}w_{\mathrm{im}}$, the imaginary part. The full $K = 128$-tap complex projection for one window and one profile is thus $64$ tap-pairs $\times$ 2 instructions, mapping exactly onto a 64-thread block with no idle lanes (the warp-exact geometry promised in Section~\ref{sec:param:tradeoffs}); the $3 \times 64$ pre-packed weight lanes occupy 768 bytes, preloaded into shared memory per block. A scalar unpack-multiply-accumulate path implementing the identical arithmetic is retained behind a compile flag as the direct-validation reference for the packed path. Figure~\ref{fig:impl:datapath} draws the whole chain once, with the two non-integer paths used only to verify it. Figure~\ref{fig:impl:bits} draws the three bit layouts everything else rests on: the packed sample byte, the DP4A lane word, and the mask word that doubles as the completion counter.

\subsection{The Frozen Fixed-Point Transform: the \texttt{fxfft} Family}
\label{sec:impl:fxfft}
The coherent integration of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:coherent} requires an FFT that is bit-identical between the production-candidate kernel and the reference --- which, per Section~\ref{sec:impl:contract}, no floating-point library can provide. The transform is therefore implemented within the project and frozen as specification \texttt{fxfft256}~v1:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Structure:} a 256-point forward DFT in the negative-exponent convention of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:coherent}, radix-2 decimation-in-time, bit-reversed load of the 128 zero-padded window sums, natural-order output, in a fixed butterfly order.
    \item \emph{Twiddles:} $W_n[k] \approx e^{-2\pi i k/n}$ in Q15 fixed point, $C[k] = \mathrm{nearest}(32768 \cos(2\pi k/n))$, $S[k] = \mathrm{nearest}(-32768 \sin(2\pi k/n))$ for $k < n/2$, frozen as \emph{source literals} --- no runtime trigonometry, and the table is checkable by hash. No entry sits at a rounding tie, so ``nearest'' is unambiguous; the extremal values $\pm 32768$ are held in 32-bit storage and are exact under the rounding rule.
    \item \emph{Butterfly:} $t = \mathrm{round15}(W[k] \cdot b)$, then $(a, b) \to (a + t,\, a - t)$ in 32-bit integers, with the complex product formed exactly in 64 bits before rounding.
    \item \emph{Rounding:} $\mathrm{round15}(v) = \lfloor (v + 2^{14}) / 2^{15} \rfloor$ per component.  This rule breaks exact half-step ties toward $+\infty$; it is neither round-to-even nor symmetric round-away-from-zero.  The verified build records the CUDA compiler version and explicit \texttt{--std=} language mode supported by that toolchain \cite{nvidiacpp}.  In C++20, right shift of a negative signed integer is specified as arithmetic division rounded toward negative infinity \cite{cppshift}; the host/device conformance gate additionally asserts \texttt{(-1 >> 1) == -1} and exercises negative half-step boundary vectors.  Older language modes are not accepted solely on the strength of an implementation convention.
    \item \emph{Scaling:} none. Correctness rests on a no-overflow lemma: one butterfly maps the working infinity norm $M$ to at most $(1 + \sqrt{2} + 2^{-15})M + 1$, so eight stages from the input contract $|{\cdot}| \le 2^{20}$ remain below $2^{30.7} < 2^{31} - 1$. Evaluated row sums obey $|{\cdot}| \le 14336 < 2^{14}$, leaving six bits of spare contract headroom; the reference implementation enforces the contract and raises on any excursion rather than wrapping.
\end{itemize}

\textit{One master table, every supported length.} The transform length is not a
free parameter of the method but a consequence of the frame and window lengths,
$L_F = 2N/K$; across the receivers and frame sizes contemplated here it takes
values from $256$ to $2048$. Rather than freeze an independent table per length,
the specification freezes a single master of $M/2$ entries and derives each
member from it. The construction is an exact identity, not a re-derivation: a
radix-2 decimation-in-time of length $n$ requires $W_n[k]$ only for $k < n/2$,
and $W_n[k] = W_M[kM/n]$ whenever $n$ divides $M$; because the Q15 rounding is
applied to the same real value either way, the length-$n$ entries are
\emph{bit-identical} decimations of the master. Adding a supported length
therefore introduces no new rounding decisions, and the absence of rounding ties
--- verified once on the master, whose closest approach to a tie is
$5.8 \times 10^{-4}$, some eight orders of magnitude above the double-precision
epsilon at that scale --- is inherited by every member. The stride folds into the
existing index arithmetic: stage $s$ reads $\mathrm{master}[\,t \ll (\log_2 M - s)\,]$,
which at $L_F = 256$ selects exactly the entries the original $128$-entry table
held. The master is generated offline and emitted as source literals for the
device, since runtime trigonometry would reintroduce precisely the
cross-platform floating-point non-determinism the frozen specification exists to
eliminate; a hash pins the table, and a build gate compares the emitted header
against the generator.

The no-overflow lemma is likewise per-length rather than inherited. Applying the
per-stage bound $\log_2 L_F$ times and requiring the result below $2^{31}$ gives
the admissible input norm, which \emph{tightens} as the transform grows:
$2^{20}$ at $L_F = 256$, then $2^{18}$, $2^{17}$ and $2^{16}$ at $512$, $1024$
and $2048$. The eight-stage contract is not reusable at nine stages. All four
admit the implemented row-sum bound of $2^{14}$, and each is asserted at compile
time against the accumulator width actually configured.

Downstream of the transform, exactness resumes unconditionally: spectral magnitudes squared and their sums over $M = 2048$ feeds are computed in unsigned 64-bit integers, with evaluated magnitudes small enough that the frame-level sums sit far below overflow. The specification is realized three times --- a numpy integer reference, a portable C port, and the CUDA device implementation --- and all three are required to reproduce a golden-vector corpus of 56 input/output pairs spanning synthetic families, survey-measured pilot offsets, and genuine \texttt{int4}-dot row-sum arithmetic, byte-for-byte. The corpus, the twiddle table hash, and the rounding rule together define the transform: any arithmetic change constitutes a new specification version.

The cost of the fixed-point rounding is measured directly. Against the analyzer's \texttt{complex128} pipeline, the frozen transform's spectrum differs by at most $6.3 \times 10^{-4}$ of the spectrum peak across all golden families, and the end-to-end fine statistic moves by at most $5.9 \times 10^{-4}$ relative ($-0.0026$~dB at a measured detection peak) --- five orders of magnitude below the coherent-integration effect the transform exists to capture. A deterministic-float alternative (fixed operation order, fused multiply--add disabled) was considered and rejected: although it can be made deterministic on a single platform, its determinism depends on compiler flags rather than on the specification itself, and it departs from the integer arithmetic on which every other verification gate in this section relies.

\subsection{Exact Rational Decision Arithmetic}
\label{sec:impl:decision}
The decision rule of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:decision} is a chain of comparisons among ratios of 64-bit integers. The implementation never forms the ratios. Each per-bin statistic is carried as an integer pair $(n_f, d_f) = (2 S_0[f],\, S_1[f] + S_2[f])$, and every comparison is evaluated by cross-multiplication in fixed-width arithmetic: $n_i / d_i < n_j / d_j$ becomes $n_i d_j < n_j d_i$, computed exactly as 128-bit products (high and low 64-bit halves) --- well within bounds, since the Parseval identity caps evaluated power sums near $2^{55}$. The rank selection uses these comparisons in a counting form: bin $i$ holds one-based rank $\rho$ iff the number of bulk bins strictly below it is less than $\rho$ while the count including ties (itself and every equal value) is at least $\rho$, that is, $n_{<} < \rho \le n_{\le}$. This formulation is order-free by construction --- the selected \emph{value} is unique even under exact ties, and because tied bins are equal as rationals, any representative yields the same downstream decision, so the result is independent of thread scheduling by construction rather than by locking. The final threshold comparison, $n_f \cdot 2^{16} \cdot d_\rho > \eta_{q16} \cdot n_\rho \cdot d_f$ with the multiplier in Q16 fixed point, is a comparison of triple products; it is evaluated in explicit 192-bit arithmetic (each side as three 64-bit limbs), exact for arbitrary 64-bit operands, independent of the operand bounds. The degenerate rules of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:decision} are implemented as stated: zero-denominator bins are excluded from the bulk and can never fire in the designated set, and a bulk not deeper than $\rho$ forces the non-rejection outcome.

The reference for this arithmetic is an arbitrary-precision implementation (Python integers), itself gated against a rational-number oracle.  The 192-bit comparison is written as explicit schoolbook limb products and carry propagation: each $64\times64$ product contributes a low and high limb, carries are added with overflow detection, and the terminal carry is required to be zero because each side is the exact product of three 64-bit operands represented in three 64-bit limbs.  Boundary vectors cover $0$, $1$, $2^{64}-1$, repeated maximal limbs, exact ties, and operands chosen to generate carries at every stage.  A fuzz gate then drives $2 \times 10^6$ pseudo-random operand sets through both the fixed-width code and the arbitrary-precision reference.  The randomized agreement is strong empirical evidence, but the completeness claim rests on the explicit limb construction and terminal-carry invariant rather than on fuzzing alone.

\subsection{The Fused Kernel}
\label{sec:impl:fused}
The production-candidate form computes the entire pipeline --- packed samples in, one mask bit out --- in a single kernel launch. The motivation is the deployment footprint of Section~\ref{sec:system:detector}: a staged implementation must materialize the $3 \times 262144$ complex row sums to device memory ($\approx 6$~MB per frame) and re-read them for the transform, while the fused form keeps each stream's row sums entirely in on-chip shared memory, touching global memory only for the $3 \times 256$ exact power sums and the final mask word.

The kernel assigns one 64-thread block per stream (2048 blocks per frame) and proceeds in four phases. \emph{Phase 1:} the block walks the window axis with a thread stride, each thread taking whole windows and computing their projections for all three profiles via the DP4A path of Section~\ref{sec:impl:datapath} --- no cross-thread reduction, and no requirement that the window count divide the block size --- writing the $3 \times L$ complex row sums to shared memory and accumulating per-thread contributions to the coarse marginals in registers. \emph{Phase 2:} the block reduces and atomically adds its coarse marginals to the frame totals --- making the exact-integer counterpart of the Parseval identity (Eq.~\eqref{eq:parseval}) an \emph{internal} property of the launch --- and, when a debug tap is bound, exports the row sums; in production the tap is null and row sums never exist off-chip. \emph{Phase 3:} the block executes the frozen transform cooperatively for each profile: bit-reversed load into a shared 256-point buffer, then eight butterfly stages of 128 butterflies spread across the 64 threads, barrier-synchronized between stages. Within a stage, butterflies write disjoint index pairs, so the produced bits are identical for any thread schedule --- the specification's fixed butterfly order is preserved not by serialization but by the disjointness of the writes. Squared magnitudes are then accumulated atomically into the global fine power sums, exact because integer addition is associative and commutative --- the accumulation \emph{order} is nondeterministic, the \emph{result} is not. \emph{Phase 4:} a per-frame completion counter folded into the mask word is incremented by each finishing block behind a memory fence; the last-arriving block observes the full count, re-reads the finalized power sums, and executes the decision arithmetic of Section~\ref{sec:impl:decision} cooperatively --- bulk census, rank selection, designated-set comparison --- overwriting the counter with the mask bit.  The public launch wrapper owns a stream-ordered zero of the mask word immediately before the launch, and the raw kernel entry point is internal.  A caller that bypasses this contract can begin from a false completion count, select the wrong finalizer, or prevent any block from observing the expected count; the outcome is therefore invalid rather than a stale-but-usable mask.  The acceptance suite deliberately pre-fills the word with a nonzero pattern and verifies that the wrapper restores correct behavior. The per-channel calibration inputs (anchor, window width, bulk mask, rank, multiplier) enter as kernel arguments from the runtime calibration bundle: the library compiles in no channel policy, so calibration campaigns modify data rather than code.

The wrapper also enforces the remaining launch preconditions: output power arrays are zeroed or freshly allocated in the same stream; calibration dimensions match the compiled geometry; all buffer spans are large enough for the declared frame; and a CUDA error or failed post-launch tripwire marks the frame invalid rather than silently retaining or rejecting it.  These are part of the API contract because the exact kernel cannot repair an incorrectly initialized host-side state.

\subsection{Verification Methodology \& Measured Performance}
\label{sec:impl:verify}
Verification is layered so that each property is established by the cheapest gate that can decide it, and every layer's criterion is exact equality. What this section describes is chosen by one rule: a check appears here only if it survives into the software someone else will rerun. There are three durable tiers. \emph{Production invariants} run on every frame, forever, and their failure is a scientific event (the marginal tripwire, reason-coded validity, the completion-counter contract). \emph{The release suite} runs on every build and every commit (\texttt{make test}, \texttt{make release-check}, \texttt{make freeze-check} in \texttt{pilot-proxy}; the corresponding targets in the other repositories of Appendix~\ref{app:software}), and passing it is what the reproducibility claim of this dissertation means. \emph{Development diagnostics} --- debug taps, ad hoc dumps, one-off comparisons made while the design was moving --- are not described unless they explain a design decision, in which case they live in Appendix~\ref{app:legacy}. Each gate below is tagged with its tier.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Frozen-specification gates} [release suite]. The 56 golden vectors pin the transform across all three implementations; the twiddle table is hash-checked; a generator cross-check reproduces the table from first principles in the test suite.
    \item \emph{Reference-chain gates} [release suite]. The decision reference is gated against a rational-number oracle across randomized, tied, degenerate, wrapped, and near-overflow cases; the wide-limb comparisons are fuzz-gated against arbitrary precision over $2 \times 10^6$ trials.
    \item \emph{Concurrency emulation} [release suite]. Before any GPU execution, the device source is compiled unmodified into a threaded CPU harness: one POSIX thread per GPU thread, a barrier for each \texttt{\_\_syncthreads}, mutexed atomics, the scalar dot path substituted for DP4A. This executes the kernel's true concurrent structure --- including the cooperative transform and the last-block epilogue --- under real thread interleaving, and its outputs are required to match the reference chain bit-for-bit across noise, injected-pilot, wrap-anchor, degenerate-bulk, and tap-unbound scenarios, stably across repeated concurrent runs.
    \item \emph{On-hardware gates} [release suite]. On the benchmark GPU, the fused kernel must reproduce the composed staged path and the reference bit-for-bit; an injected pilot must fire the mask; and twenty repeated launches must produce identical bits --- the one property the CPU emulation cannot probe, since it exercises the completion-counter epilogue under genuine grid scheduling. (Two optional debug taps exist behind compile flags for development; production binds them to \texttt{NULL}, and the suite checks that binding them changes no output.)
    \item \emph{Production tripwire} [production invariant]. The exact marginal identity is the acceptance tripwire for a live integration: the coarse power sums are computed twice, through independent code paths, and compared as integers every frame. Any fault in memory layout, accumulation, scheduling, or a future regression breaks an exact equality on the frame where it occurs.
    \item \emph{Cross-build reproduction} [release check]. Two independently built processing cohorts (different hosts, compiler builds, and analyzer source states) process an overlapping archive subset, and their per-frame integer outputs are joined on the physical archive key and compared as integers. The claim is stated with its denominator: joined events and frames, join completeness, invalid and missing records, the exact fields compared, and both immutable build manifests. Under a tolerance regime that comparison would require a distributional judgment; under the exactness contract each joined field reduces to equality.
    \stub{v5 archive rerun}{Cross-build comparison record: the two build manifests, the joined event/frame denominator and completeness, and the mismatch count (expected zero) over the compared integer fields.}
    \item \emph{The build is not the artifact} [release check]. One further finding completes the procedure: compiled binaries are \emph{not} byte-deterministic --- clean rebuilds of identical sources differ in embedded compiler artifacts. The verified binary itself, content-addressed by cryptographic hash and recorded in the processing ledger, therefore serves as the reference artifact; a rebuild is a new artifact that must re-pass the verification gates before use.
\end{enumerate}

Measured performance on the benchmark-class GPU (A100) is summarized as follows. Against the $41.94$~ms frame cadence, the staged three-launch composition costs $2.14$~ms; the fused kernel completes the samples-to-powers datapath in $0.77$~ms (a $55\times$ real-time margin), with the debug tap adding only $\approx 5~\mu$s when bound; and the full production-candidate form including the decision epilogue costs $0.90$~ms --- a $47\times$ margin, with the entire decision stage contributing $+0.13$~ms because only one block per frame executes it. The complete evaluated detector therefore occupies approximately $2\%$ of its real-time budget.

\section{Synthetic Detection Calibration on the LimeSDR}
\label{sec:detection:synthetic}
\label{sec:impl:legacyinjection}

The synthetic campaign of Section~\ref{sec:estimator:validation} validated estimation without a threshold. Detection needs three further results from the same platform, all at the production geometry ($K = 128$, $L = 128$, $M = 2048$, the fine designated-set statistic, and the exact Q16 decision): the sensitivity of the fine decision against the coarse one, the representation loss of the packed fixed-point path against a full-precision control on the \emph{same} trials, and the mask-versus-residual frontier of Eq.~\eqref{eq:detection:frontier} evaluated against known injected contamination. The obstacle is the spatial axis. A LimeSDR has one receive chain, and the quantity that sets the detector's null width and deflection is the number of independent inputs summed non-coherently (Section~\ref{sec:estimator:statistics}).

\subsection*{Building the spatial axis from independent captures}

The non-coherent feed sum makes this tractable. Every operation upstream of $S_r[f]$ --- projection, row sums, transform, squared magnitude --- is applied to each input independently, and the inputs enter only through the power sum $S_r[f] = \sum_m |\tilde B_m[f,r]|^2$ of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:noncoherent}. Under $H_0$ the analytic model treats the $M$ inputs as independent noise looks; under $H_1$ the same pilot frequency appears in every input with its own amplitude and phase, which is exactly the nuisance structure $a_m e^{j\phi_m}$ of the $H_1$ model in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline:problem}, and the noncentrality accumulates as $\sum_m |a_m|^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:estimator:statistics}). A frame assembled from $M$ \emph{independent captures} of the same over-the-air setup is therefore a controlled independent-input pseudo-array --- not a realization of the telescope, whose feeds see one simultaneous waveform through a covariance, gain structure, and sky the bench does not have, but an exact realization of the analytic i.i.d.\ model: independent receiver noise per input, a common pilot with per-input phase, and --- because the detector is applied to the assembled $M \times N$ frame as a whole --- the production kernel with all $2048$ blocks, atomic sums, and last-block epilogue exercised on real radio-path samples. Four constructions are used, in order of what each establishes:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Independent-capture stacking (the endpoint).} At each injected shelf SNR and pilot offset, a continuous capture of $M$ frame-lengths (about $86$~s at $41.94$~ms per frame) is sliced into $M$ non-overlapping $N$-sample segments and stacked as the $M$ inputs of one frame. The mixture is formed digitally, as in the estimator sweep: one over-the-air signal capture supplies the pilot and shelf, independent noise-only captures supply each segment's noise, and the two are combined at the commanded SNR, so the noise captures are reused across SNR points and only the signal segments need re-recording per offset. Because the same board transmits and receives, the pilot's baseband frequency is clock-common across segments, as it is across real feeds; a per-segment anchor check confirms it. Further trials draw fresh captures, and bootstrap intervals resample whole captures, never individual inputs. This is the measurement the campaign reports, and it is a measurement, not a scaling.
\item \emph{The $M$-sweep (the law).} The same captures are re-assembled at $M \in \{1, 2, 4, \dots, 2048\}$, and the null width, its robust core, and the deflection of the fine and coarse statistics are measured at each $M$. The idealized model predicts null width $\propto 1/\sqrt{\beta M}$ and deflection $\propto \sqrt{M}$; the sweep shows where the measured detector follows that law and where quantization, the transform's rounding, or capture non-stationarity bends it, so that the $M = 2048$ endpoint is read from a curve rather than from a single point. A single-chain result scaled analytically by $\sqrt{2048}$ is recorded beside the sweep as the cross-check it is.
\item \emph{The fully correlated bracket (the other extreme).} One capture replicated across all $M$ inputs gives the opposite limit --- perfectly correlated noise, effective degrees of freedom $\beta$ rather than $\beta M$ --- and the widest null the geometry can produce. Real feed covariance on a telescope lies between (i) and (iii); the archive width factors of Table~\ref{tab:calibration:nulls} locate where.
\item \emph{The paired implementation control.} Every trial carries the packed-int4 production statistic and a ladder of controls evaluated on the \emph{same} samples (Figure~\ref{fig:detection:paired}): floating arithmetic with full-precision weights; floating arithmetic with the quantized production weights; the fixed-point CPU oracle; and the production GPU path --- with an unquantized-input branch beside them where the int4 quantizer's own cost is to be isolated. Consecutive rungs isolate weight quantization, the frozen transform's rounding, and input quantization from one another, and representation loss is measured as a paired horizontal shift of detection crossings, never inferred from independently generated curves. The waveform is audited on its stationary span before use (an earlier campaign found a generator startup transient that moved the apparent pilot ratio by several tenths of a decibel; Appendix~\ref{app:legacy}).
\end{enumerate}

One waveform-and-noise realization is quantized once and evaluated by every arithmetic path, so that the differences between paths are attributable to representation alone: full-precision-weight float to quantized-weight float isolates weight quantization, quantized-weight float to the fixed-point oracle isolates the frozen transform's rounding, and the oracle to the GPU must be bit-identical. Two properties of this construction should be stated plainly. It does not reproduce a telescope's feed covariance, sky signal, or gain structure --- that is what the archive null measures, and Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline:modelcal} draws the boundary between the two layers --- and it does not test propagation or multipath. What it establishes is the detector's behaviour under the analytic model on real radio-path data at the full production geometry, with the fixed-point cost isolated by pairing.

\subsection*{What the synthetic campaign can and cannot decide}

The operating point of the offline and live detectors is selected against a science residual tolerance (Section~\ref{sec:tolerance:thresholds}), not against a false-alarm rate, and no bench radio has a science tolerance; the synthetic campaign therefore selects nothing. Fixed-$P_{\rm fa}$ detection curves appear in it for exactly one purpose: a representation loss is only measurable as the horizontal shift of a crossing, so $P_{\rm d}$ against shelf SNR at a fixed $P_{\rm fa}$ is the ruler for the paired implementation comparison, and nothing quoted from it is a threshold. What the campaign \emph{can} decide is the part of the selection machinery the archive can never check. RFIsher's selector consumes the residual-score histogram family of Eq.~\eqref{eq:detection:histogram} and returns a point on the frontier of Eq.~\eqref{eq:detection:frontier}; the frontier itself --- masked fraction against the contamination that survives in the kept frames at each $(\rho,\eta)$ --- requires no tolerance to draw, and on the bench the surviving contamination is \emph{known}, frame by frame, from the injection. The campaign therefore builds the histogram family from labelled trials and compares what the cumulative sums claim survives with what was injected, which validates the preparation bookkeeping against truth before it is applied to an archive whose truth is unknown.

The labelled trials come from a transmitter duty cycle: the pilot and shelf are switched on and off at random across frames at declared duty cycles, so the off frames form a null population whose membership is known rather than inferred, the on frames carry a known shelf, and the same frames yield the exchangeability check of Section~\ref{sec:calibration:nulls} with truth labels. The synthetic counterpart of the archive plates follows from the same trials: the frame-averaged spectrum of all frames beside that of the frames the mask keeps, at each duty cycle. One limit of that picture must be understood rather than worked around. A shelf $40$~dB below the noise contributes $4\times10^{-4}$~dB to an averaged floor, invisible in any spectrum at any practical frame count; in that regime the kept-frame residual is reported from the injected truth, which is exactly why the detector exists --- it decides on a pilot the spectrum cannot show. The before-and-after spectra therefore demonstrate the loud regime and the pilot line; the frontier from injected truth carries the sub-noise regime.

\section{Offline Archive Detection}
\label{sec:detection:archive}
\label{sec:pipeline:example}

The offline protocol is the same for every one of the 23 allocations, and it is executed on the survey products of Chapter~\ref{sec:survey}, which retain the exact integer terms of every frame. It has five steps, and each produces one per-channel plate or table row.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Identify the latest stable era.} The fine-statistic time--frequency record of each channel (a monthly spectrogram of the fine $F$ statistic) is thresholded to locate transmitter switch-ons, sign-offs, and station changes; the latest interval with one accepted station state and one accepted instrument state is the channel's current era, and everything downstream uses only its frames (Section~\ref{sec:calibration:eras}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration:eras}).
\item \emph{Characterize the era.} The time-averaged spectrum of the current era's frames around the pilot locates the anchor and shows how far the transmitter population spreads relative to the fine span (Figure~\ref{fig:census:psd}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration:anchors}); the histogram of the era's frames gives the null distribution, calibrated on its bulk as median plus left-side scale rather than on the mean of the mixture (Section~\ref{sec:calibration:nulls}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration:nulls}).
\item \emph{Select the operating point.} RFIsher receives the era's residual-score histogram family $\{\mathcal{H}_\rho\}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:detection:histogram} and the science tolerance, and returns $(\rho^\star, \eta^\star)$ by the cost rule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:detection:frontier} --- one threshold per channel, with the rank as an optional second degree of freedom (Section~\ref{sec:tolerance:thresholds}, Table~\ref{tab:tolerance:eta}).
\item \emph{Apply it and show what it removed.} The selected pair is applied to every frame of the era, and three normalizations of the frame spectra $P_i$ are kept apart because they answer different questions: the \emph{all-frame mean} $\bar P_{\rm all} = \sum_{\rm all} P_i / N$; the \emph{retained conditional mean} $\bar P_{\rm keep|keep} = \sum_{\rm keep} P_i / N_{\rm keep}$, what a kept frame looks like; and the \emph{retained contribution} $\bar P_{\rm keep,contrib} = \sum_{\rm keep} P_i / N$, what survives into the integration. What the mask removes is $\bar P_{\rm all} - \bar P_{\rm keep,contrib}$, evaluated in linear power --- not the difference of the two means, whose denominators differ. The plates show $\bar P_{\rm all}$ and $\bar P_{\rm keep|keep}$; the residual chain consumes $\bar P_{\rm keep,contrib}$. Conversion to decibels is the last operation in every case (Appendix~\ref{app:archive-diagnostics}).
\item \emph{Hold out.} The era is split by whole acquisition, sidereal day, or contiguous time block, never by frame; the pair is frozen on the calibration blocks and applied once to the disjoint evaluation blocks without retuning, and the report gives masked fraction, retained residual, $r_{\rm sys}/r_{\rm tol}$, empirical false-alarm rate wherever a verified off state exists, invalid and coverage counts, and block-bootstrap intervals (Section~\ref{sec:survey:holdout}). An era change, absent valid null, missing transfer, or zero retained frames produces a refusal rather than an inferred operating point.
\end{enumerate}

Steps 1--4 are the per-channel results this dissertation reports; step 5 is the gate between an in-sample operating point and an operational recommendation. Because the archive informed development, a blocked split of the same captures establishes temporal transfer rather than a pristine blind result; the closing claim uses blocks reserved before the operating points were selected.

\section{Kotekan Stage and Live Detection on the CHORD Pathfinder}
\label{sec:detection:runtime}

The Pathfinder stage takes the estimators of Chapter~\ref{sec:estimator}, binds one runtime-bundle entry by coarse-channel identity, and emits one rejection byte plus the exact target, lower-reference, and upper-reference marginals per integration. In automatic mode it applies the fine designated-set rule only for a bundle whose fine-calibration status is accepted; the production configuration refuses an uncalibrated pilot channel rather than silently falling back to the survey flag, which was never a policy. Its synthetic GPU oracle and file-pipeline replay establish arithmetic and transport parity with the offline reference (Chapter~\ref{sec:deployment}).

The live detection test is a predeclared hours-long shadow run in which those decisions are recorded but do not alter downstream data. The run records bundle identity, per-frame validity and decision, estimator outputs, exactness-tripwire state, dropped and late frames, runtime quantiles, anchor and bulk drift, and time-block summaries of occupancy and retained proxy residual --- and, for each monitored channel, the frame-averaged spectrum of all frames and of the kept frames over the run, the live counterpart of the archive plates of Appendix~\ref{app:archive-diagnostics}. Sampled frames are replayed through the independent offline path, and the locked live summaries are compared with the offline predictions. Acceptance requires exact replay on the sampled inputs, no silent frame loss, latency and memory headroom at live cadence, stable calibration behaviour, and a declared response to known state changes. Only a successful shadow run can authorize a later applied-mask trial.

The measured \rerun{$0.90$-ms} kernel time establishes computational readiness against the $41.94$-ms frame cadence; it is not online validation. Chapter~\ref{sec:deployment} states the interface contract the stage must meet and reports the run.
